The neuron and class challenge rows are each challenge's second measurement in the run, when the

dearest a dispute gets.

`docs/numbers.md` for the shipped model) plus 96 bytes for its three immutable fields.

### What the numbers show

- **A dispute is priced by the one neuron it re-runs.** The two models have a layer of the same
  fan-in (784): challenging one of its neurons costs 144,462 gas in the small model and 150,803 in the large
  one, +4.4%, while the large model has 4.3 times the parameters and its whole forward pass costs
  4.33 times as much (8,219,318 against 1,896,043).
  The fan-in is the same, so the kernel does the same number of multiply-adds; the 6,341 gas of
  difference were not broken down further. What differs: the calldata is 160 bytes longer and all of
  it is hashed, because the layer's output vector is 4 packed words instead of 1 and the proof of the neuron
  measured has 8 nodes instead of 6. The record also has one more slot to load.
- **Unflattering: below a few hundred inputs the fan-in hardly matters, because the fixed part of a
  challenge dominates.** In the large model the challenges cost 150,803 (fan-in 784), 81,262 (128) and 79,968 (64).
  From 784 to 128 inputs the cost falls by 69,541 gas, about 106 per input (arithmetic, calldata and
  hashing of the row and the input together); from 128 to 64 it falls by only 1,294, and the smaller
  one even has more calldata (804 bytes against 740: the last layer's output is 10 `int32` words, a
  hidden layer's is packed). The cheapest challenge, `challengeClass`, runs no neuron and costs 65,882:
  storage, the commitment check and the transaction itself. So a dispute is never much cheaper than
  that, however small the neuron.
- **Unflattering: the claim does grow with the model, by its width and its depth.** `claim` carries
  every layer's output, so its calldata went from 1,188 to 1,348 bytes (+160) and its gas from 246,113 to 274,284
  (+28,171, +11.4%): one more committed vector is one more hash in a fresh storage slot (22,100 gas) plus
  its calldata and its copy in the `Claimed` event. It grows with the sum of the layer widths, not with
  the number of weights: 4.3 times the parameters cost 1.11 times the claim gas.
- **The saving grows with the model.** The honest path (`claim` + `finalize`) is 15.6% of one whole
  forward pass for the small model and 4.0% for the large one; the dearest dispute is 9.4% and 2.3%.
  In gas, not in shares: the honest path costs 30,108 more for the large model and the dearest dispute
  6,341 more, against 6,323,275 more for the whole forward pass.
  The whole forward pass of the large model is 49.0% of the per-transaction gas cap, so a network about
  twice this size could no longer be run on-chain in one transaction at all, while its claims and
  disputes would cost about what these do. That last sentence is an inference from the table, not a
  measurement: no third model was built.
- **Code size grows by one handler per layer:** the `Arbiter` went from 11,745 to 14,457 bytes.
