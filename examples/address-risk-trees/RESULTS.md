Whole-forest runs measured: sample 0, 1,301,202 gas (1,279,842 of execution); sample 6, 1,296,863 gas (1,275,503 of execution).
"Later slashes" are each challenge's second measurement in a run, when the challenger's credit
and the `burned` total are already non-zero; the first tree challenge, 118,880 gas, is the first slash
ever and also turns those two slots from zero to non-zero. Tree 299 has a proof of 6 siblings,
not 9: with 301 leaves the last nodes are promoted unchanged up the Merkle tree.

### What the numbers show

- **The honest path is cheaper than one whole run.** One whole run is 1,301,202 gas; claiming and
  finalising is 251,287, 19.3% of it. That is the opposite of the dense network on the same data, whose
  honest path costs 181.8% of its whole run: the forest is bigger to run (every tree is loaded and
  walked) while the claim grows only by its committed outputs (19 words). The answer still
  arrives only after the challenge window; a contract that needs it at once calls `Ensemble`.
- **A dispute stays small:** the dearest one (`challengeTree (tree 5, proof 9)`) costs 118,880 gas, 9.1% of a whole run. Walking one
  tree is 6 comparisons; the rest of a tree challenge is the fixed cost of a slash (hashing the two
  committed vectors, the Merkle proof, the storage writes of the settlement).
- **Per tree, the whole run costs about 4,266 gas of execution** (1,279,842 over 300 trees, including the
  calls to the holders and the sum). A tree challenge replaces all of that with one walk.
- **In absolute gas a tree dispute costs more than a neuron dispute of the small dense network**
  (118,880 against 101,727 gas, the dearest of each). Its calldata is 1,316 bytes against 452: the tree's
  8 words, a proof of 9 siblings and the 19 committed output words, which are also hashed.
  Against what each model costs to run whole, the tree dispute is the cheaper by far (9.1%
  against 65.7%).
- **The forest is the better model on these rows** by F1 (0.7065 against 0.6422) and accuracy (91.22%
  against 89.35%), as is common for tabular data. It is also about 37 times the dense network's size
  (counting a node and a leaf as one parameter each), which is why running it whole costs 840.9%
  of the dense network's whole run.

## 3. The use, end to end: `RiskGate` on anvil

`gate_anvil.sh`: an operator claims the forest's answer for two accounts' features (reference samples
0, cleared, and 6, flagged), finalises both after the window, and scores the accounts in
`RiskGate` (`ingots/consumer`, hand-written, 1,051 bytes deployed). The cleared account enters; the
flagged one and an unscored one are refused. Scoring from an open claim, scoring by anyone but the
operator, and clearing one account with the claim about another's features all revert; the script
checks each.

| Call | Forest: gas | Calldata bytes | Dense example: gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| claim (cleared sample) | 204,576 | 676 | 230,399 |
| claim (flagged sample) | 187,356 | 676 | 212,910 |
| finalize (cleared sample) | 63,811 | 36 | 68,062 |
| finalize (flagged sample) | 46,723 | 36 | 50,974 |
| score A (cleared) | 89,070 | 100 | 93,552 |
| score B (flagged) | 89,060 | 100 | 93,542 |
| enter (A, cleared) | 46,989 | 4 | 46,989 |

So one account's standing costs the operator 323,139 gas (the second claim, its finalize and its score;
the first of each kind in a run costs more because it also sets a storage slot for the first time).
Calling `Ensemble.classify` instead is 1,301,202 gas (section 2); a gate built on that call would add its
own storage write, and was not built or measured here.

an honest claim survives the challenge of every one of its 300 trees and the class challenge; a wrong
